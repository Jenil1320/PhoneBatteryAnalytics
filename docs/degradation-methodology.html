Cellspan · Methodology

How Cellspan calculates battery degradation

Cellspan shows a modeled — “typical” — State of Health beside the value it measures from your phone, so you can tell whether your battery is aging normally for its cycle count. Here is exactly how that modeled number is computed, and where the parameters come from.

Model: Schmalstieg NMC / SimSES Calendar + cyclic Reference: Google Pixel 7

01The idea

Capacity loss is modeled as the sum of two independent processes and subtracted from a healthy 100%. One process runs with time and temperature even when the phone rests (calendar aging); the other runs with charge throughput as you use and recharge it (cyclic aging).

SoHmodeled = 100% − ( Qcalendar + Qcyclic )

The equations and coefficients are the Schmalstieg holistic NMC aging model as implemented in SimSES, the open-source energy-storage simulator from the Technical University of Munich. Of the models available it is the closest to a consumer lithium-polymer phone cell: an NMC/graphite chemistry with a sloped voltage curve, and — unusually — it is parameterised in cell voltage, which a phone can actually measure.

02The two terms

Each term is a stress factor multiplied by an aging curve. Loss is a per-unit fraction; multiply by 100 for a percentage.

Calendar · time & heat

Rest aging grows with √-ish time

Qcal = αcap · tdays0.75
αcap = (7.543·V − 23.75)·106 · e(−6976 / T)

Driven by an Arrhenius temperature term (T in kelvin) and a linear voltage term. Growth follows t0.75 — fast early, then slowing — as the SEI layer thickens on the anode.

Cyclic · throughput

Use aging grows with √-throughput

Qcyc = βcap · √Qthroughput
βcap = 7.348·10−3(V−3.667)2 + 7.6·10−4 + 4.081·10−3·DoD

A parabola in mean cell voltage (gentlest at 3.667 V) plus a linear depth-of-discharge term, times the square root of charge throughput in ampere-hours.

Charge throughput is the total ampere-hours passed through the cell: equivalent full cycles × design capacity. For the Pixel 7, that is the device-reported cycle count × 4.355 Ah.

04Calibration check

Anchored to the Pixel 7

Google rates the Pixel 7 for about 80% capacity after roughly 800 charge cycles. With a typical phone cycle-mean voltage (~3.7 V) and depth of discharge, the cyclic term alone predicts ≈ 20% loss at 800 equivalent full cycles → ≈ 80% modeled SoH — independently matching the manufacturer figure. That agreement is the model's sanity check, and it is asserted by a unit test in the app.

05What the phone can and can't feed it

Two deliberate input choices keep the model honest on real hardware.

Voltage is not taken from the live reading. Both terms want a mean voltage the phone can't cleanly observe — the cycle mean for the cyclic term, the rest mean for calendar. The recent sample average is dominated by charging voltage (~4.3–4.45 V), which pushes the parabola far off and over-predicts wear. Cellspan uses fixed representative values instead, so cyclic wear is driven by throughput (cycle count), which is what actually accumulates it.

Calendar aging needs a birth date the sandbox hides. A normal app cannot read the manufacture date (it is SELinux-blocked), so on a stock phone only the cyclic term contributes and the app shows “needs battery age” for the calendar part. With root or an ADB helper, the calendar term switches on using the measured mean temperature.

06Why it's labeled “typical”, not exact

The coefficients are fit to a 4.2 V-max NMC 18650 cell. Modern phones charge to 4.4–4.45 V and many use LCO or LCO/NMC pouch cells, where high-voltage degradation accelerates beyond the fitted range; the Pixel 7 also actively lowers its charge ceiling over life. So the model's shape and exponents transfer — square-root-of-time calendar aging, square-root-of-throughput cyclic aging, Arrhenius temperature — but the absolute coefficients are approximate for a phone cell. Cellspan therefore presents the output as a typical reference for comparison, never as an exact or manufacturer-accurate State of Health. The number you should trust for your specific battery is the measured one; the modeled value is there to tell you whether the measured value is normal.

07Forecasting the future

Cellspan also projects where your battery is heading. This is on-device trend analysis — a plain statistical model that runs on your phone in a few lines of arithmetic — not "AI", and every figure is an estimate with an honest range, never a guarantee.

State of health · to 80%

A trend line, checked against physics

SoH ≈ a + b·cycles  (recency-weighted)

A recency-weighted least-squares regression of your measured SoH against charge cycles — a textbook machine-learning fit, run on-device. Its 80% crossing is converted to a date from your cycles-per-day, cross-checked against the physics model (fitted with a single scale factor that says whether you age faster or slower than typical), and the earlier of the two crossings is reported so the estimate isn't optimistic near end of life. The range comes from the slope's standard error.

Drain · rest of day

Projected from your recent rate

SoC(t) = SoCnow − rate · Δt

Time-to-empty and the dashed rest-of-day curve come from the recent discharge slope (percentage points per hour), smoothed to avoid jumps. "Typical daily drain" is the median of recent days, which shrugs off the occasional heavy day. Shown only while actually on battery.

When there isn't enough history yet, the forecast falls back to the physics model alone and says so. The wording is deliberately hedged — "may reach 80% around …", "at your recent usage" — because battery behavior shifts with temperature, charging habits, and how you use the phone.
